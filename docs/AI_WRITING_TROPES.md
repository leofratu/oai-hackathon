# AI writing tropes to avoid

Source: [tropes.fyi](https://tropes.fyi)

Use this checklist when writing UI copy, documentation, submission text, or release notes. A pattern used once may be fine. Repetition and combinations make prose sound generated.

## Word choice

- Avoid magic adverbs used to manufacture significance: quietly, deeply, fundamentally, remarkably, arguably.
- Avoid stock AI verbs and adjectives when plain words work: delve, certainly, utilize, leverage as a verb, robust, streamline, harness.
- Avoid ornate domain nouns: tapestry, landscape, paradigm, synergy, ecosystem, framework.
- Use "is" and "are" instead of pompous substitutes such as "serves as", "stands as", "marks", or "represents".

## Sentence structure

- Limit negative parallelism such as "It is not X; it is Y" and "not because X, but because Y".
- Avoid dramatic countdowns built from fragments: "Not X. Not Y. Just Z."
- Do not ask and immediately answer rhetorical questions such as "The result? Disaster."
- Avoid repeating the same sentence opening several times in a row.
- Do not stack several three-part lists or slogan-like contrasts.
- Remove filler transitions such as "It is worth noting", "Importantly", "Interestingly", and "Notably".
- Remove shallow present-participle endings such as "highlighting its importance" or "underscoring its role".
- Use "from X to Y" only for a real range.
- Avoid runs of gerund fragments such as "Shipping faster. Moving quicker. Delivering more."

## Paragraph structure

- Avoid strings of tiny paragraphs or fragments used for manufactured emphasis.
- Use a real list when the content is a list. Do not disguise a list as paragraphs beginning with "The first", "The second", and "The third".

## Tone

- Avoid false-suspense transitions: "Here is the kicker", "Here is the thing", and "Here is where it gets interesting".
- Explain the subject directly instead of starting with "Think of it as" or a patronizing analogy.
- Do not open speculative sales copy with "Imagine a world where".
- Avoid performative vulnerability such as "And yes, if we are being honest".
- Prove a claim instead of declaring that "the truth is simple" or "history is clear".
- Keep the stakes proportional. Do not present routine product work as a historic shift.
- Avoid teacherly setup such as "Let us break this down", "unpack", "explore", or "dive in".
- Name sources. Do not attribute claims to unnamed experts, observers, or reports.
- Do not invent analytical labels by attaching words such as paradox, trap, creep, divide, vacuum, or inversion to a topic.

## Formatting

- Limit em dashes. Use commas, parentheses, semicolons, or separate sentences where appropriate.
- Do not start every bullet with a bold label.
- Avoid decorative Unicode arrows, smart quotes, and similar characters in source text. Prefer plain ASCII such as `->` when an arrow is necessary.

## Composition

- Do not preview, repeat, and summarize the same point at every level.
- Use a metaphor once and move on.
- Avoid stacking historical company or technology analogies to imply authority.
- Make each section add information. Do not restate one argument in several forms to increase length.
- Check for duplicated paragraphs or sections.
- Do not announce the conclusion with "In conclusion", "To sum up", or "In summary".
- Avoid the formula "Despite its strengths, X faces challenges" followed by an automatic optimistic ending.

## Editing pass

Before shipping prose:

1. Replace inflated words with specific nouns and verbs.
2. Remove filler sentences that add no fact or instruction.
3. Merge fragments into normal sentences.
4. Cut repeated claims and repeated metaphors.
5. Replace vague attribution with a named source or remove the claim.
6. Check punctuation for excess em dashes, smart quotes, and decorative symbols.
7. Read the copy aloud. It should sound like a person explaining the actual product.
